## The Short Answer

**Buy AHD if** you need the lowest total cost per camera, you are wiring a new building where you want cheap reliable cable, and you only need local recording on a monitor or phone app.

**Buy IP if** you want better image quality at higher resolutions, remote access over the internet without extra equipment, and a system that will not need replacing in three years.

Both are current, both are stocked in Qatar, and both are legitimate choices. The difference is cost, cable type, and how you view footage.

## What Is AHD?

AHD (Analog High Definition) is a CCTV standard that sends video over traditional coaxial cable — the same RG59 or RG6 cable used for older analog systems. A DVR receives the signal, decodes it, and records it.

The important thing about AHD today: it is **not** the old blurry analog video. Modern AHD runs at 2MP, 5MP and above, and the picture quality at 5MP is genuinely competitive with 4MP IP at a fraction of the price.

## What Is an IP Camera?

An IP (Internet Protocol) camera digitises video at the camera and sends it as data over Cat6 — the same cable used for office networking. That data goes to an NVR, and because it is ordinary network data, you can view it from your phone anywhere in the world without any extra hardware.

This is why IP costs more. The camera has a processor and network interface built in.

## The Real Cost Comparison

A single camera is misleading — total system cost is what matters.

**4-camera AHD system:**
- 4 × 5MP AHD cameras: 680 QAR
- 4CH hybrid DVR: 416 QAR
- 4 × 100m RG59+2C cable runs: ~340 QAR
- 12V power supply: ~40 QAR
- **Total: ~1,480 QAR**

**4-camera IP PoE system:**
- 4 × 8MP IP cameras: 3,400 QAR
- 8CH 4K NVR: 760 QAR
- 4-port PoE switch: 200 QAR
- 4 × 100m Cat6 cable runs: ~400 QAR
- **Total: ~4,760 QAR**

AHD is roughly one third of the cost. That gap is real and it is the whole reason AHD still sells well in Qatar for shops, small offices and warehouses.

## Where IP Wins Decisively

**Remote access.** With IP cameras on an NVR, you open an app and see your property from anywhere. With AHD, you need an HDMI extender or IP encoder to bridge the DVR onto your network. That extra device costs money and adds a point of failure.

**Resolution ceiling.** AHD tops out around 5MP in practice. IP cameras go to 8MP, 12MP and beyond. If you need to read a license plate from 20 metres or identify a face across a warehouse, only IP gets you there.

**One cable does everything.** A PoE camera needs a single Cat6 run for video, power and network. AHD needs coax for video plus a separate power pair or a nearby outlet. Fewer cables means faster installation and fewer failures.

**Upgradability.** The network infrastructure behind IP cameras (switch, router, cabling) is useful for everything else in the building. The coax behind AHD is not.

## The Qatar-Specific Factor

Qatar's heat is the deciding issue in outdoor installs. Coaxial cable and connectors degrade faster in sustained high temperature, and a DVR in a hot utility room is a heat problem. IP gear runs cooler and is easier to cool in a rack, and a rack-mount NVR with proper airflow is straightforward to plan for.

**Practical rule for Qatar:** if the cameras will be mounted on a villa perimeter, a construction site or an outdoor compound, IP with PoE is worth the extra cost. If they are indoor in an air-conditioned shop or office, AHD is the value choice.

## Can You Mix AHD and IP Cameras?

Yes — on a **hybrid** recorder. A hybrid DVR/NVR accepts both analogue-over-coax and IP cameras on the same unit. This is the cheapest way to build a system in stages, or to replace old analog cameras one at a time instead of all at once.

Two things to check before you buy:

1. The recorder must be hybrid, not a plain DVR or plain NVR.
2. Confirm the maximum channel count is split correctly — a "16-channel" hybrid recorder may allow 8 AHD + 8 IP rather than 16 of either.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is AHD the same as old analog CCTV?**
No. AHD is a digital signal carried over coax. Modern 5MP AHD is close to 4MP IP in clarity. What it lacks is IP's networking features, not image quality at comparable resolutions.

**Can I run AHD on the cable already in my building?**
Usually yes, if it is RG59 or RG6. Run a test with a short length first — very old or very thin cable can drop the signal at 5MP.

**Do I need a PoE switch for IP cameras?**
You do, unless the NVR has built-in PoE ports. Check the NVR before adding a switch — it saves both cost and rack space.

**Will my old DVR work with new IP cameras?**
No. IP cameras need an NVR. A DVR cannot connect to a network camera directly.

**Which is better for night footage?**
Neither type is inherently better. It comes down to the sensor and IR illumination in the specific camera. Check lux rating and IR range, not the technology label.
