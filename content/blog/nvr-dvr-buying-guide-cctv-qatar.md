## What Is a Security Recorder (NVR or DVR)?

A security recorder is the central unit that stores footage from all your cameras, powers them, and lets you review recordings. It replaces the need for individual microSD cards in each camera.

There are two types:

- **NVR (Network Video Recorder)** — works with IP cameras (cameras that connect over your network via Ethernet cable)
- **DVR (Digital Video Recorder)** — works with analog cameras (cameras that connect via coaxial cable like RG59 or RG6)

Your camera type determines which recorder you need. You cannot mix IP cameras with a DVR, or analog cameras with an NVR, unless you use a hybrid unit.

## NVR vs DVR — What's the Difference?

| Feature | NVR (Network) | DVR (Analog) | Hybrid (NVR + DVR) |
|:---|---|:---|:---|
| Camera type | IP cameras (Ethernet) | Analog cameras (coaxial) | Both IP + analog |
| Video quality | Up to 12MP (4K+) | Up to 5MP (most common) | Mixed resolutions |
| Cable type | Cat6 Ethernet | Coaxial (RG59/RG6) | Both |
| Power delivery | PoE (power over the same cable) | Separate power cable per camera | PoE + separate power |
| Installation complexity | Single cable per camera | Two cables per camera (video + power) | Mixed |
| Best for | New installations, 4K systems | Upgrading old analog systems | Mixed camera types |
| Price | Higher | Lower | Highest |

**Rule of thumb:** If you are buying cameras today, choose IP cameras with an NVR. Analog is being phased out globally. IP gives higher resolution, single-cable installation, and remote access.

## How Many Channels Do You Need?

| Number of Cameras | Recorder Channels | Recommended |
|:---|---:|:---|
| 1-3 cameras | 4-channel | Small home setup, leave 1-2 spare ports |
| 4-7 cameras | 8-channel | Villa or small office |
| 8-15 cameras | 16-channel | Large villa, medium office, school |
| 16-23 cameras | 16 or 32-channel | Business, warehouse, multiple buildings |

**Rule:** Always buy a recorder with more channels than you need today. Adding a camera later is simple; replacing the entire recorder to add 2 more cameras is expensive.

## How Much Storage (HDD) Do You Need?

The amount of footage you can store depends on resolution, frame rate, and how many cameras record simultaneously.

| Resolution | Per Camera Per Day (24h recording) | Per Camera Per Day (motion only) |
|:---|---:|---:|
| 1080p (2MP) | 10-20 GB | 3-6 GB |
| 4MP | 20-40 GB | 6-12 GB |
| 4K (8MP) | 40-80 GB | 12-24 GB |

**Example calculation:** 8 cameras at 4MP recording motion only:
- 8 cameras × 8 GB/day = 64 GB/day
- A 1 TB HDD stores approximately 15 days of footage
- A 2 TB HDD stores approximately 30 days

**For most businesses in Qatar:** 1 TB per 4-8 cameras is sufficient for 2-4 weeks of motion-triggered recording. Most NVRs and DVRs support up to 10 TB HDD (single drive).

## PoE vs Non-PoE NVR

| Feature | PoE NVR | Non-PoE NVR |
|:---|---|:---|
| Camera power | NVR powers cameras via Ethernet | Cameras need separate power adapters |
| Cabling | One Cat6 cable per camera | Cat6 + power adapter per camera |
| Install location | NVR can be in a different room (PoE limit: 100m) | NVR location limited by power access |
| Best for | New installations, clean setup | When cameras are far from NVR (need PoE extender) or existing cabling |

For villas and offices, a PoE NVR is the standard choice. One cable per camera carries power, video, and audio — installation is clean and fast.

## Key Features to Look For

### Resolution Support
Ensure the recorder supports the resolution of your cameras. An NVR advertised as "4K" may support 4K input but record at lower resolution to save bandwidth. Check the recording resolution, not just the display resolution.

### Remote Viewing App
Every modern recorder has a mobile app. Check that the app works reliably and supports playback, not just live view. The Secuview app supports both live view and recorded playback from anywhere.

### Audio Support
If your cameras have built-in microphones, the recorder must support audio recording. Some recorders drop audio by default — check that audio is enabled per channel.

### Motion Detection Zones
The recorder should let you define specific areas within each camera's view for motion detection. Ignoring street traffic while alerting on a gate entry is essential for reducing false alerts.

## Common Questions

**Can I use my existing analog cameras with an NVR?**
No. Analog cameras use a different signal format. You need either a DVR or a hybrid recorder that supports both analog and IP cameras.

**How far can cameras be from the NVR?**
IP cameras over Cat6 cable work up to 100 meters. Beyond that, use a PoE extender or switch between the camera and NVR. Analog cameras over coaxial cable can reach 200-300 meters depending on cable quality.

**Can I access my cameras when I am outside Qatar?**
Yes. The NVR connects to your router, and the mobile app works from anywhere in the world with internet. You do not need a static IP — the recorder uses P2P (peer-to-peer) technology to connect automatically.

**Do I need a monitor connected to the NVR?**
No. You can set up the NVR entirely through the mobile app or a web browser. A monitor is useful for initial configuration but not required for daily use.

**What happens if the NVR hard drive fails?**
The recorder logs an error and stops recording. Replace the HDD with a surveillance-rated drive (WD Purple or Seagate SkyHawk). These are designed for 24/7 continuous write operations, unlike desktop drives.

**Can I mix 1080p and 4K cameras on the same NVR?**
Yes. Most NVRs support mixed resolutions. Each camera records at its native resolution. The recorder does not downsample higher-resolution cameras to match lower ones.
